Beaconhouse Nastaliq is the first open-source Urdu Nastaliq typeface, designed and engineered entirely by developers in Pakistan. Commissioned by Beaconhouse Group and designed and engineered by Muhammad Zeeshan Nasar, it brings the visual character of Noori Nastaliq to a modern, lightweight, character-based architecture suited for the web, publishing, and education.

Unlike traditional Nastaliq fonts that rely on thousands of pre-composed ligatures, Beaconhouse Nastaliq uses only about 600 glyphs in total, including Latin glyphs. This was made possible by a custom joining-glyph technique: a set of extension glyphs that combine with base letterforms through OpenType GSUB rules to produce the hundreds of contextual forms the script requires, without pre-drawing each one individually.

One of the hardest problems in digital Nastaliq is dot collision between adjacent letters. Beaconhouse Nastaliq resolves this automatically through custom GPOS and GSUB rules, and includes full support for Urdu diacritics (اعراب) for accurate, readable text at any size.

The font also includes a distinctive educational feature: its glyphs are shaped and cut so that each letter's contribution to a joined word can be visually isolated, for example by coloring individual characters in a joined word. This makes it a practical tool for children learning how Urdu letters connect to one another.
